How it works

Where every number comes from, what it means, how missing values are handled, and how the map areas are drawn.

Principles EQAO results Comparisons Codes Students & enrolment Map areas Updates Sources

Principles

  1. Every number links to its source and shows its school year.
  2. Context, not leaderboards. Each school is shown next to its board and the province, over several years. There are no “top 10” lists and no single score.
  3. Honest about gaps. Withheld, missing and small-group values are labelled, never shown as 0.
  4. Boundaries are approximate unless official. Every map layer says what it is.
  5. Free and private. No accounts, ads, tracking cookies or third-party scripts.

EQAO results

The Education Quality and Accountability Office (EQAO) assesses every student in Grade 3 and Grade 6 (reading, writing, mathematics), Grade 9 (mathematics) and Grade 10 (the Ontario Secondary School Literacy Test, OSSLT). For each school we show the percentage of students who met the provincial standard (level 3 or 4; for the OSSLT, students who passed on their first attempt), exactly as published in the Ministry’s School information and student demographics files.

  • Years shown: {{ meta.eqao_years|join(", ") }}. EQAO did not assess students in 2019-20 or 2020-21. The Ministry’s files for those two years repeat 2018-19 results, so we use them only for school details and student context, never as new results.
  • Grade 9 math was split into “academic” and “applied” courses until 2018-19. Since 2021-22 there is one de-streamed course. We show them as separate measures and never join them into one trend line.
  • The Ministry randomly rounds some school percentages up or down to protect privacy, so 0% and 100% may not be exact.

Board and Ontario comparisons

Next to each school we show two comparison lines, computed by this site from the same Ministry file and year:

  • Board median: the middle value among the board’s schools that have a result for that assessment and year (shown only when at least 3 schools have one; the number of schools is in each table).
  • Ontario median: the middle value among all schools of the same language (English-language or French-language schools write different versions of the assessments).

A median of schools is not the same as the official percentage of all students in a board or the province — a large school and a small school count the same. Where the Ministry publishes an official board result (Grade 6 reading and the OSSLT, from School board achievements and progress), we show it separately and label it “official”.

Codes for missing or withheld values

We never turn a code into a number. A coded value is stored as empty (null) with a flag, and the page shows the code.

Student population and enrolment

The “About the students” figures (first language, newcomers, special education, gifted, low-income households and parents’ education) come from the latest Ministry file ({{ meta.latest_sif }}). The last two are estimates the Ministry builds by matching student postal codes to Statistics Canada census data. Values are suppressed (SP) when fewer than 10 students are represented.

Enrolment comes from the Ministry’s Ontario public schools enrolment files (2011-12 onward). Since 2018-19 the Ministry rounds school enrolment to the nearest 5; schools with fewer than 10 students show <10.

Map areas

  1. Postal code area (first 3 characters) — Statistics Canada’s 2021 forward sortation area (FSA) outlines. We check every school: the postal-code FSA matches the outline that contains the school’s point for {{ fsa_check_rate }} of {{ "{:,}".format(meta.fsa_check.checked) }} schools ({{ meta.fsa_check.mismatches }} mismatches, listed in the pipeline report). Pages group schools by their postal code.
  2. Approximate school board area — the Ministry’s Board service area file is listed as “under review” with nothing to download, so we cannot use it. Instead, for each of the four systems, we merge the nearest-school areas (below) of all of a board’s regular schools. This shows roughly where a board’s schools are; it is not its legal jurisdiction.
  3. Nearest school, NOT the official boundary — for each system and level, the province is split so every place belongs to the school it is closest to in a straight line (a Voronoi diagram), clipped to Ontario’s land. Schools that share a building share one area. Alternative, adult, online and similar special-purpose schools are left out. Real attendance areas follow roads, rivers, capacity and board decisions, so they can look very different.
  4. Official boundary from a board — not used yet. We will add a board’s attendance areas only where it publishes them openly or gives written permission, labelled with the board and date.

Distances (“nearest other school”) are straight-line distances between published school coordinates. Schools with no coordinates in any source year were placed by matching their address with the Natural Resources Canada Geolocation Service; those pages say so.

Updates

Data last updated {{ meta.data_updated_human }}. The pipeline re-downloads every source, checks every column it reads, and stops with a clear message if a file’s layout changes — it never guesses. A full report of record counts, mismatches and suppressed values is published with the downloads.

Services the site calls from your browser

  • Map tiles from OpenFreeMap, built from OpenStreetMap data (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). Loaded only when a map is shown.
  • Address search (only when you search for a street address) uses the Natural Resources Canada Geolocation Service. We send just the text you typed, at most once every two seconds.

Not used

Third-party school rankings, real-estate listings, or any data whose terms forbid reuse. We may link to them, but never copy them.
